### Round 1 — Phase 5 only — 2026-08-18 — fresh three-lens panel, repo access — **NOT READY, ESCALATED**

**Round 0, asked once for this episode: does this phase produce the observable, and is it
the right one? Yes.** §1 says this spec's observable is `llk-001`'s — the drawn map — and
that every phase is judged on whether a real network comes out the far end drawn; Phase 5
draws a second real city. It is the right one because `llk-001` OQ-2 has demanded this
measurement three times and §4 is explicit that a question with no phase attached is one
nothing will ever force.

A panel of three — correctness/grounding, exit-gate testability, scope/cross-file
consistency. **All three returned NOT READY.** Escalated to the human after round 1 rather
than adjudicated into rounds 2 and 3, because the finding is not that the phase is written
badly: **round 1 falsified the premise the feed was chosen on**, and choosing the feed is
the human's call, not the loop's.

**The escalating finding, measured rather than reasoned.** The grounding reviewer reached
the CTA site, which the author could not, and measured the live archive:
`google_transit.zip` is **99,567,748 bytes** — 111× BART's 892,312 — with `stop_times.txt`
at **373 MB uncompressed, ~6.16 M rows**, and `shapes.txt` a further 52 MB. Independently
confirmed by the author against the live `content-length`. Two consequences the phase
never names:

- `llika-gtfs/src/feed.rs:Feed::read` collects every table whole, and its own doc comment
  defers the case: "a city's `stop_times.txt` running to hundreds of megabytes is a named
  Phase 4 hazard rather than this phase's problem." **Phase 5 is the feed where it fires**,
  and the fix is a streaming reader — a phase, not a repair.
- OQ-4 decided BART's commit on **two** grounds, licensing and repository weight, and
  discharged the weight half at 892 KB. `bart.md` forbids trimming a snapshot ("The bytes
  are BART's, unmodified"), so the precedent Phase 5 inherits makes a ~95 MB commit
  mandatory.

**And the snapshot is 17 days wide.** CTA's `calendar.txt` runs `20260805`–`20260822`;
`last-modified` is 2026-08-06. BART's committed copy spans 2026-01-12 → 2026-08-30. The
phase asks `chicago.md` to mirror `bart.md`'s refresh discipline, under which every
hand-counted literal *and* (per `llk-001` Phase 7's amendment) the criteria vector move on
every refresh — at ~95 MB of permanent git history each time. CTA also ships no
`feed_info.txt`, so gate 1's "published validity" has no field to read.

**The phase's lead argument is also wrong, independently of the feed, and two lenses
caught it separately.** "A ring core reaches two branches of `llk-001` §2.5 that have never
executed" fails three ways: the quoted "Not reachable on the committed fixture" belongs to
§2.5's *(line, position-in-list)* bullet, not to the two run-endpoint exceptions, of which
§2.5 says the opposite — "legal input rather than corner cases, **both reachable**", with
"totality rather than a picture" at stake; `corridor.rs`'s
`a_closed_run_is_directed_from_its_lowest_station_towards_its_lower_neighbour` already
asserts `direct_closed_run`, the shared fallback for both shapes, so "neither branch has an
assertion behind it" is false; and the **no-endpoints branch is unreachable by
construction** on a ring *with branches* — any line entering the ring drives that station
to degree ≥ 3, making it a collapse station — so the phase's own bullets 1 and 2 assert
incompatible things about the same network. What survives is narrower and true: neither
committed fixture contains such a run, and the *integration* path has no coverage.

**Gates 3, 4 and 5 cannot fail.** Gate 3 asks a test to observe that a renderer branch was
reached, but `llika-core/src/render/corridor.rs` is a private module whose own test header
records that "nothing in `llika-core/tests/` can reach into" it — and it offers both
outcomes as passes. Gate 4 names no relation that must hold and no literal that must match,
and does not say whether the campaign is a committed test or a one-off measurement; the two
readings differ by minutes of suite time at ~143 stations. Gate 5 states no threshold, no
method, and needs machinery the tree does not have — there is no `Instant`, no `criterion`
and no `[[bench]]` anywhere, and its 0.13 s BART baseline is pre-Phase-7 and roughly 2× off
at the shipped weights.

**Two hazards were falsified and the real one was missing.** CTA publishes **eight** rail
routes at `route_type = 1`, one per line, with no `Yellow-N`/`Yellow-S` direction split — so
`draws_the_same_line` has no candidate pair to misfire on. Meanwhile the import-side scale
hazard, the one certain to fire, is absent from the list. The layout half is probably fine:
~143 V / ~150 E is seconds, not 72.9 s.

**Also confirmed correct, and worth keeping:** §2.4's cycle/cluster claim verbatim and
`cluster.rs:find`'s behaviour; Phase 7's `w_crossings` limit; `draws_the_same_line`'s
predicate; `ImportParams::default()` = `[0, 1]`, and CTA carrying no `route_type = 0` so the
default works as on BART; §2.1 rejecting only *consecutive* repeats; 72.9 s at 200, 0.13 s
on BART, BART 50/6; CTA ≈143 rail parent stations against "roughly 145", with 0 rows missing
coordinates; and the CTA licence quotes verbatim, redistribution granted in terms — the
licence ships *inside* the archive, so verification is cheaper than the phase assumed.

**§6.1's ordered test was worked independently and the author's answer holds**: append to
`llk-002`, not `llk-001` and not a new spec. Both cross-spec writes are correctly directed
and their precedent is real. The boundary drawn with a future `llk-001` Phase 8 is coherent.
None of that is what escalated.

**Nothing was folded in.** With the feed under question the phase's scope, hazards and four
of its seven gates all move, and folding first would be polishing a document about to be
rewritten. `phases[4].reviewed` stays `null`, per §7.7 — the date records convergence, and
this round did not converge.

### Round 3 — document-wide — 2026-08-15 — same reviewer resumed — **READY**

Zero blocking findings; converged in three rounds, inside the cap.

Both round-2 blockers verified fixed **at the root rather than at the gate** — the
missing properties went into OQ-5's list, which is the one list this spec argues must
not grow late, rather than into the assertions that needed them. The round re-derived
the interleaving case independently: at Phase 1 the array is `[B, A_p1, A_p2, C]`, and
at Phase 2 the parent-row rule gives `[A, B, C]` where the first-platform rule gives
`[B, A, C]`, so the two genuinely diverge and the assertion can fail for a wrong
implementation.

**The round did the thing that makes it trustworthy: it built a witness.** Asked whether
one fixture feed can satisfy all eleven of OQ-5's now-interacting properties
simultaneously — the risk round 2's fixes created — it constructed the feed rather than
reasoning about it, and checked every pair that touches the same table. No two properties
are in tension. The interleaving property and the parentless-platform property turn out to
be satisfiable by the *same* station. The cost is a fixture of ~10 routes and ~14 stops,
past "four small CSVs" but hand-authorable in one sitting, and that is recorded rather
than discovered while authoring it.

It also separated two things this document had conflated. The **line** count carries from
Phase 1 to Phase 2 as an exact delta — `L2 = L1 − 1`, since the short route is the only
route that can drop — while the **station** count carries only as a relation, strictly
below, because its exact delta is not derivable from the spec. That asymmetry is now
deliberate and explained rather than accidental.

Five non-blocking findings, all folded in: a route dropped under OQ-3 is now stated to
contribute no stations, closing a one-station ambiguity in a literal Phase 2 commits to;
OQ-5 now fixes **exactly one** colourless kept route, since a second would move the
palette index Phase 1 asserts; Phase 1's naive trip rule gained the `trips.txt` row-order
tie-break it will need at Phase 3 anyway; Phase 1's two ordered-list literals now name a
route whose representative trip is stable across OQ-1, so they do not churn at Phase 3;
and Phase 2's gate 5 now asserts the binary's exit status, which was the only thing OQ-3
still had open and the one thing the gate did not check.

**All four phases take the `reviewed` date, and the reason is the round's scope.** §7 says
a spec with no shipped phase takes one document-wide round, and this reviewer judged all
four phases — Phase 2's and Phase 3's gates and Phase 4's hazards each drew findings. A
date on Phase 1 alone would mean the document-wide round bought nothing for the rest.
§7's rule that editing a phase's scope clears its `reviewed` back to `null` is the safety
valve, and Phases 2–4 are the likely users of it once Phase 1 ships and measures something.

`status` moves to `accepted`. Phase 1 is cleared to build.

emit-iff-referenced rule across §2.1, Phase 1 and Phase 2 rather than accepting it as

claimed: because OQ-5 requires a split-platform station served by two routes, Phase 2's

**Two new blocking findings, both created by round 1's own fixes**, and both the same
shape: round 1 added two concrete Phase 2 assertions without adding the fixture

1. **Phase 2's `LineTooShort` gate could not fire.** Deflating OQ-3 to that one
   condition turned a vague gate into a concrete one, and the fixture had no route that
   produces it. The consecutive-platforms trip cannot double as it, because Phase 2's

2. **Phase 2's emission-position gate was vacuous as the fixture would naturally be
   authored.** Under the grouped layout `A_parent, A_p1, A_p2, B, C` the parent-row rule

station in the Gulf of Guinea and drags the network's centroid with it.

### Round 1 — document-wide — 2026-08-15 — fresh reviewer, repo access — **NOT READY**

**Round 0 — is this the right thing to build at all?** Yes. It produces no observable of
its own and §1 argues that explicitly rather than assuming it: it produces the *input* to
`llk-001`'s SVG, and every phase is judged on whether a real network comes out drawn. And
it is the right one — `llk-001` §1.1 deferred real-network import, its OQ-4 left the method
open, the project has never drawn anything but its own 17-station fixture, and `llk-001`'s
OQ-2 says in terms that what would settle its provisional weights is "a second network with
a different shape", which nothing else in the corpus can supply.

Scope note: `llk-002` has no shipped phase, so per §7 this is one document-wide round
covering all four phases rather than a phase-scoped one, and one review episode.

Four blocking findings, seventeen non-blocking. **The code grounding passed clean** — the
reviewer opened every cited `file:symbol` and found no stale or wrong citation, and the
quoted `llk-001` passages reproduce verbatim. What blocked it was the layer the spec's own
`reference:` field flags as unvalidated.

1. **§2.1 and §2.4 stated GTFS wrongly, and Phase 1's parser is built from that table.**
   `stop_lat`/`stop_lon` are Conditionally Required, not required; `location_type` has
   five values, not two, and 2/3/4 are exactly the rows that may carry no coordinates. A
   parser typed off the reflex reading hard-fails on any feed with entrances. Closed with
   a nullability column, an `Option` rule, and a read-only-0-and-1 rule.
2. **Which `stops.txt` rows become stations was never stated, and §2.1 and Phase 1 gave
   different answers.** One reading emits parent rows as stray isolated markers, the other
   does not; they give different station counts and a different picture. Closed with one
   rule — a station is emitted iff a kept line references it, identity current — which
   holds unchanged across both phases.
3. **Phase 1's `stop_sequence` assertion was vacuous.** Non-consecutive values in
   ascending row order are read identically by a correct reader and by one that ignores
   `stop_sequence` entirely — the one behaviour §2.1 goes out of its way to mandate. Only
   rows written *out* of sequence order discriminate. The sharpest catch of the round.
4. **"A missing or unusable colour" was undefined and §2.4 contradicted §1.1.** GTFS
   defaults an omitted `route_color` to `FFFFFF`, so "missing" and "white" are one value
   downstream and two cells in the file. Closed with an exact predicate and a fully
   specified palette, and in §1.1's direction: an explicitly white line stays white,
   because §1.1 assigns that editorial call to the person.

One finding was **partially rejected**. The round read OQ-5 as a decision wearing an open
question's name, and asked for it to be `RESOLVED` or moved into §2. Rejected on
precedent: `llk-001`'s own OQ-5 has exactly this shape — it fixed its fixture's required
properties inside the question and was marked `RESOLVED` by the phase that authored the
file. What was accepted is that the settledness was not signposted, and that Phase 3's tie
case and single-trip route were missing from the list.

Of the sixteen other non-blocking, two closed questions outright. The reviewer *ran*
`{"stations":[],"lines":[]}` through the shipped `llika` — exit 0, valid 160×160 SVG — which
closed half of OQ-2 against the code rather than by inference, per §4's rule that
code-answerable questions are answered during review. And it found §1's illustrative cost
pair repeating `llk-001` §1's own invented-numbers failure, one spec later; §1 now says so
in terms and names Phase 4 as where the figures are replaced.
